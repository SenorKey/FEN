    Notes on Method

    What this comparison is.

    Preside by Side documents instances of presidential misconduct, legal jeopardy, policy failure, and overreach drawn from primary government sources, court records, inspector general reports, and established journalism. It is not a full accounting of any presidency. Every president featured here also holds a record of legislative achievements, policy successes, and historical contributions that this project does not weigh. The intent is a structured look at the documented faults of these administrations, not a verdict on the people who led them.

    On the framing of this project.

    Readers will undoubtedly notice that Preside by Side is presented in a deliberately charged, instigative frame. Its setup is certainly incendiary by design. That provocation is presentational, not editorial. Beneath it, the project aims to document the significant and relevant negative actions of all presidents, regardless of party affiliation or personal philosophy. The goal that matters is that each bar rests on an unbiased, quality, source-backed description; the charged packaging does not lower the standard the underlying evidence is held to.

    How events are selected.

    Events are included when they are supported by credible primary or institutional sources (congressional findings, federal court rulings, agency inspector general reports, or established reporting from recognized outlets). Political controversy, contested opinion, and events without documented factual grounding are excluded. Each bar links to its primary sources so readers can evaluate the evidence directly rather than taking this project's framing on faith.

    How severity is rated.

    Severity scores (1–10) were assigned with AI assistance, weighing each event's scale of harm, legal significance, and lasting consequence by present-day standards (an act like slaveholding drew little controversy in its era, yet is rated severely here because the rights it violated are now held as absolute). These are a best-faith estimate, not an authoritative judgment: AI tools carry biases, scores can reasonably be disputed, and where you'd weigh things differently, the linked sources are there for you to do so.

    On visual symmetry.

    Each side of the comparison displays the same number of bars as a design decision. It keeps the layout balanced and consistent across all possible pairings. It is not an editorial claim that any two presidents' actions are equivalent in their conduct, the gravity of their records, or their place in history. The bar lengths, driven by severity scores, carry the comparative weight; the count does not.

    Sources and corrections.

    All source links point to publicly available primary documents or institutional reporting as of the dates cited. If you believe a source is mischaracterized, a severity score is clearly indefensible, or a significant documented event has been omitted, this project welcomes that feedback.

    What's planned next.

    Preside by Side is an evolving project. Planned additions include reader voting on severity, a way to register where you'd weigh an event differently from its AI-assisted score, and a suggestion box for documented events or topics you feel the project has overlooked. The goal is to keep the comparison accountable to the evidence and open to correction, rather than fixed on any one reading of the record.
