      Beyond the price

      Look up a symbol above to see what its last filings reported.

      Market cap
      — What the whole company costs at today’s price: every share there is, multiplied by the last price.
      P/E ratio
      — Price divided by a year of earnings per share — roughly, the years of current profit you are paying for one share. Blank when the company lost money, because the ratio stops meaning anything.
      Earnings per share
      — Profit over the last four reported quarters, divided among every share.
      Dividend yield
      — A year of declared dividends as a percentage of the price. Blank means the company declared none, not that it paid nothing.
      Beta
      — How far this moved on an average day the market moved 1%. Measured against SPY, the proxy the tiles at the top use. Above 1 is jumpier than the market; below 1 is steadier.
      Shares outstanding
      — How many shares exist. It is the number that turns a price into a company’s worth, and a falling one means the company is buying its own back.
      Revenue
      — Everything the company sold over the last four reported quarters, before any cost is taken off.
      Gross margin
      — What is left of each sale once the cost of making the thing is paid.
      Operating margin
      — What is left after the cost of running the business as well — wages, research, rent.
      Net margin
      — What is left after everything, including tax and interest. The three margins always fall in this order.

      How this works

      Nothing here is real money.

      You get a fake $100,000 balance. You cannot deposit, withdraw, win or lose anything. There is no account, no sign-up and no email — your portfolio is stored in this browser and nowhere else.

      Prices are delayed, and orders fill forward.

      Free market data runs behind the real market. That would make a trading game trivially easy, so an order fills at the next price the server fetches after you submit it, never the price you were looking at when you clicked.
